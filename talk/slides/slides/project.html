Most research isn't one question, though. It's a whole project: many questions, many thrusts. That's the second scale, and it has two skills: one to develop the project, one to audit it. organize-research-project is the development side. Research means pushing many thrusts in different directions, and most of them fail. So each thrust lives in its own study, an isolated folder, and inside each study you use exactly the one-question skills, investigate-conjectures and solve-math-rigorously. My AGENTS.md tells every agent to stay inside its own study, because once an agent picks up a wrong approach, it tends to follow it forever. But pure isolation means reinventing the wheel, so there's also an established core of theory and code. A result gets in only through a promotion gate: an independent audit, integration with the shared notation, tests and interfaces, and my approval. That core is where the signal is high, and when I read something there, it's worth reading properly. Then, when the project becomes a paper, review-ai-paper is the audit side. A fresh reviewer gets the finished paper, not its history: it forgets how the work was made, pulls the cited sources, audits the code, and builds a claim ledger. And its findings can start the next round.
