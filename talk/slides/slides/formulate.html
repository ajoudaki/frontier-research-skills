Frozen: a material change needs your say.

Let's start with formulate-problem. A research question usually starts vague, like this cloud. The danger is that a vague question leaves room for answers to an easier question, and an agent will happily take them. The goal is the steelman, not the straw man. So the skill writes a research contract, and each field rules something out. The question itself is pinned down, so an easier target doesn't count. The definitions are fixed, so they can't drift halfway through. The quantifiers keep their order, so 'for all' can't quietly become 'there exists'. The evidence standard says what counts, so a proxy metric doesn't. And the scope is explicit, so one example can't stand in for a whole class. Notice that the vague boundary couldn't tell us whether any of these were in or out. Once they're ruled out, the sharp boundary emerges: one precise conjecture. Then it's frozen. A material change needs your say, and results for a changed question never count as answers to the original. So now we have the right question, but we haven't solved it yet. That's the second stage.
