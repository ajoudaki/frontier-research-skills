Frozen

Here's one thrust. In reality, the target isn't a sharp node at all. It's a vague question, and a lot of time goes into pinning down what we even want. The danger is that a vague question leaves room for answers to an easier question, and an agent will happily take them. The goal is the steelman, not the straw man: the strongest version that stays faithful to what you meant. So formulate-problem writes a research contract, and each field rules something out. The question itself is pinned down, so an easier target doesn't count. The definitions are fixed, so they can't drift halfway through. The quantifiers keep their order, so 'for all' can't quietly become 'there exists'. The evidence standard says what counts, so a proxy metric doesn't. And the scope is explicit, so one example can't stand in for a whole class. Notice that the vague boundary couldn't tell us whether any of these were in or out. Once they're ruled out, the true boundary emerges: one precise conjecture, with a sharp edge. Then it's frozen. A material change needs your say, and results for a changed question never count as answers to the original. Now we know exactly where the arrow points.
