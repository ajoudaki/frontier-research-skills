This next one is, I think, at the heart of why research with these models is so hard. They seem trained to solve precise problems. Give them an open question, and they find a special case, or a counterexample, or an assumption you didn't explicitly forbid, and they solve that instead. Little tweaks that kill the question. And in long threads, they slowly drift away from it. So I split the work into two stages, with two different skills. Stage one is investigate-conjectures. If your question is already precise, you freeze it as a contract that the agents can always check themselves against. If it's vague, and that's the trick I like most, you use the model's own intelligence to formulate it, faithful to the spirit of your question, with no easy escape route. The skill's research contract names ten ways a formulation can cheat, like swapping in an easier target, weakening a "for all", or proving one example of a claim about a whole class. And if the question really has to change, the original stays frozen, and results for the new question are never passed off as answers to the old one. Stage two is solve-math-rigorously. Only once the question is locked does it try to solve it, and it's told to take the strongest reasonable reading, never a straw-man version, and to show every step. That saves you the endless back-and-forth of "this isn't what I asked" and "that's cheating".
