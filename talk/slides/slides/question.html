This next one is, I think, at the heart of why research with these models is so hard. They seem trained to solve precise problems. Give them an open question, and they find a special case, or a counterexample, or an assumption you didn't explicitly forbid, and they solve that instead. Little tweaks that kill the question. And in long threads, they slowly drift away from it. If your question is already precise, you freeze it as a contract that the agents can always check themselves against. But often you don't know the right formulation yet. And here's the trick I like most: in stage one, you use the model's own intelligence to formulate the problem, faithful to the spirit of your question, with no easy escape route. Only in stage two does it try to solve it rigorously. That saves you the endless back-and-forth of "this isn't what I asked" and "that's cheating". This is the investigate-conjectures skill. Its research contract names ten ways a formulation can cheat, like swapping in an easier target, weakening a "for all", or proving one example of a claim about a whole class. And if the question really has to change, the original stays frozen, and results for the new question are never passed off as answers to the old one.
