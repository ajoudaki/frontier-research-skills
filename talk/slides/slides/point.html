05 · One question

Formulate, solve, then learn.

Even many agents downgrade an open question, drift from it, and lose it between hand-offs.

1 · Formulate

formulate-problem

Precise: freeze it as a contract.

Vague: formulate it, faithful to the spirit, and check it for ways to cheat.

Before a claim counts: separate checks rebuild every step and hunt for counterexamples.

2 · Solve

solve-problem

Only once the question is locked.

The strongest reading, never a straw man.

Every step shown; every cited theorem's assumptions checked.

3 · Learn

teach-material

Teaches the result, or any paper, one block at a time.

Every symbol defined before it's used.

Confused? It rebuilds the explanation, not a one-line patch.

We start with a single, targeted question. Even a well-scoped research question is usually still open, not exactly pinned down, and when you give it to AI agents it very often gets downgraded. An agent finds a special case or a counterexample by exploiting an assumption you didn't forbid, or solves a much weaker subproblem and presents it as the main result. Little tweaks that kill the question. With many agents it can get worse: the mistakes propagate and reinforce each other, and the goal drifts from what you intended. So the first skill, formulate-problem, formulates the problem before any attempt is made. It finds a precise formulation, faithful to your original question, with no lazy way to solve it without solving the real problem, and freezes it as a contract. Then the second skill, solve-problem, takes the strongest reading of that problem, tries to fully resolve it, and audits the proposed solution before presenting it. After these two stages, it's much more likely that we solved the right problem, and that the proof is correct. But we still have to understand it, as efficiently as possible, and the output can be very hard to read: dozens of symbols encoding something simple, in weird notation. That's the third skill, teach-material. It teaches me the result one block at a time, defines every symbol before it's used, and when I'm confused, it rebuilds the explanation instead of patching it. That's one question. Now let's zoom out to a whole project.
