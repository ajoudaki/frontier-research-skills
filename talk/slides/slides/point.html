05 · One question

Formulate first, then solve.

Even many agents downgrade an open question, drift from it, and lose it between hand-offs.

1 · Formulate

investigate-conjectures

Precise: freeze it as a contract.

Vague: formulate it, faithful to the spirit, and check it for ways to cheat.

Before a claim counts: separate checks rebuild every step and hunt for counterexamples.

2 · Solve

solve-math-rigorously

Only once the question is locked.

The strongest reading, never a straw man.

Every step shown; every cited theorem's assumptions checked.

Ways to cheat it checks for:

an easier target

a weakened “for all”

a proxy

one example for a class

We start with a targeted question. Even a well-scoped research question is usually still open, not exactly pinned down, and when you give it to AI agents it very often gets downgraded. An agent finds a special case or a counterexample by exploiting an assumption you didn't forbid, or solves a much weaker subproblem and presents it as the main result. Little tweaks that kill the question. With many agents it can get worse: the mistakes propagate and reinforce each other, and the goal drifts from what you intended. So the first skill, investigate-conjectures, formulates the problem before any attempt is made. It uses its own intelligence to find a precise formulation, faithful to your original question, with no lazy way to solve it without solving the real problem. What you get isn't a solution. It's a sharpened conjecture, frozen as a contract. Then the second skill, solve-math-rigorously, takes the strongest reading of that problem, tries to fully resolve it, and audits the proposed solution before presenting it.
