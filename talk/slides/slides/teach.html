A correct proof you don't understand isn't worth much. Here's the kind of line an agent, or a paper, might hand you: private symbols everywhere. teach-material, together with explain-canonically, teaches it in layers. First, notation. It translates into the symbols the field already uses: the parameters theta, the loss L, its gradient. Only a few global symbols, each defined with its type before it's used. Then the idea, before any detail: every step goes downhill, and if the slope can't change too fast, each step lowers the loss by a definite amount. That's also the plan of the proof. Then a toy example that keeps the real structure: a one-dimensional quadratic, where you can see exactly when the iterates shrink to zero. And only then the details. When the argument invokes a theorem, it's stated right there, in the exact form needed, with its hypothesis checked and its conclusion spelled out for our objects. And if you're confused at any point, it rewrites the whole block rather than patching a sentence. Once you understand it, you can decide whether it belongs in the core.
