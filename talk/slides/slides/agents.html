Here's the idea I think most people underestimate: many agents are fundamentally more powerful than one long thread. And it's not about speed. You pay for the same tokens either way, and the provider rate-limits you, so running agents in parallel doesn't really make you faster. The difference is conceptual. A single thread accumulates everything: every dead end, every earlier claim, your own enthusiasm. Once it makes a mistake early, everything after is anchored to it, and asking it to "be unbiased" or "start fresh" doesn't undo that. It rarely recovers. Separate agents are different by construction. Each one starts clean and sees only what you put in its folder, so you decide what it knows. That makes their judgments genuinely independent: three of them can miss a flaw, and it only takes one to catch it. So your chances multiply instead of being stuck with one. You can also give them roles that one thread can't hold at the same time: one proposes, one attacks, one checks the code. And they can explore genuinely different routes instead of all converging on the first idea. It's the difference between asking one person ten times and asking ten people once. Two of the skills are built on this: investigate-conjectures runs independent proof routes and independent audits, and organize-research-project decides who sees what, so an independent attempt or reviewer starts fresh, without earlier verdicts.
