Many agents deserve their own slide, because here's one conceptual point I think many people still underestimate: multiple agents are fundamentally more powerful than one long thread. Not because they're faster. You pay for the tokens either way, and you're rate-limited. The difference is conceptual. A single thread accumulates everything: every dead end, every earlier claim, every sentiment, enthusiasm or pessimism. Once it makes an early mistake, everything after it is anchored to it, and telling it to be unbiased can't undo that. It doesn't know how. Separate agents get a clean, independent start, especially if you isolate them and decide mechanically what they see. Each one explores its own branch, and a branch can split when it finds something worth following. Run four agents, ideally prompted for diversity. The first one misses the flaw. So does the second. The third catches it. The fourth misses again. And that's still a success: one catch is all you need. It's asking ten people instead of asking one person ten times.
