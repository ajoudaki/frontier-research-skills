How do we implement all this in practice?

The answer is a multi-agent engine: many agents, organized well, are what make a push into the research frontier feasible. It has four parts. First, why not one long thread? It accumulates everything: every dead end, every earlier claim, every sentiment. Once it makes an early mistake, everything after it is anchored to it, and telling it to be unbiased can't undo that. Independent agents start fresh, isolated, from different angles. The first one misses the flaw. So does the second. And the third. The fourth catches it, and one catch is all you need. That's how the skills run parallel proof routes and independent reviews. Agents can also work against each other. An author makes a claim. A critic objects. The author repairs it. And the critic checks again, round after round, until the objections are resolved. A single thread can't argue with itself like that. And it has to be cost-effective. Think of it as a pyramid. You're at the top: judgment and approval. Just below you, the most capable and most expensive models, for what's mission-critical: reviews, final checks, the hardest steps. Below them, capable models for drafting and derivations; at the base, cheaper models for code and routine runs. The pyramid is built so that a failure at a lower level gets caught by the audits above it.
