# Studies, continuity, and isolation

## Give the scientific investigation a durable home

A study is a bounded question or research direction. Several tasks may work on
one study, and one task may coordinate several studies with separate scientific
contexts. Conversation continuity alone does not determine study ownership.

Use an explicitly assigned study. Otherwise, a materially different question
normally starts a new study; corrections, implementation, and validation under
the same question normally continue it. Consult the project's chosen rule and
ownership metadata. Do not inspect other studies' findings merely to decide
where the current work belongs.

Keep the question, model or scope, permitted inputs, contributors, and write
assignments in the study's current record. One README is usually sufficient.
Link to proofs, source, configurations, review reports, and generated evidence
instead of duplicating their contents. Preserve useful existing ledgers; do not
mandate additional files because another skill uses the word "ledger."

## Declare the information boundary

The default inputs are the study's own material, the maintained theory and code,
their designated reproduction inputs, and authorized external sources.
Unpromoted results from another study are not shared established knowledge.

Cross-study use must follow an explicit project policy or user authorization.
If authorized, record the exact dependency, its version and provisional status,
and the resulting limits on independence. Never conceal that transfer through
chat summaries, Git history, generated arrays, or another agent. If sharing is
not permitted, report the missing dependency and keep the boundary intact.

Joint development and independent verification have different information
requirements. Shared data may be a declared common input; access to another
attempt's conclusions can defeat an independence claim. A coordinator with
access to both attempts is not thereby an independent reviewer.

## Scope work by role

| Role | Scientific reading scope |
|---|---|
| Study author | Assigned study, applicable established sources, declared external inputs |
| Bounded worker | Supplied question and designated sources; report missing inputs |
| Independent attempt | Fresh context with only the agreed problem and common inputs |
| Independent reviewer | Frozen candidate and complete dependencies, without author discussion or prior verdicts |
| Read-only question | Sources needed for that question within its assigned scope |
| Project coordinator | Permitted coordination records; scientific contents only when assigned |

Required operating instructions remain available. A narrowly scoped worker does
not need the project's entire research history. For independent work, avoid
inherited conversations and exposure to other attempts until the candidate or
attempt is frozen. Record accidental exposure and qualify the review or use a
fresh reviewer when the required independence was lost.

An assignment states its target, allowed inputs, output paths, forbidden
changes, verification method, and budget when relevant. Delegation remains
subject to the user's and environment's permissions; this workflow does not
authorize spawning agents or running additional experiments.

## Keep three statuses distinct

- Scientific status: exact, proved under stated assumptions, empirical,
  conjectural, open, contradicted, or another precise classification.
- Checking status: unchecked, internally checked, independently reviewed, or
  blocked, with evidence of the checks actually performed.
- Adoption status: exploratory, proposed for integration, accepted into the
  maintained corpus, or withdrawn.

A proof can be correct without yet being adopted. A structural check or an
author's PASS is not independent acceptance. A changed statement or dependency
can invalidate a previous check even when the filename stays the same.

## Resume, correct, and close

Resume from the current record and inspect the underlying argument or evidence
before relying on a summary. Check scope, versions, unresolved objections, and
remaining authorization. Recency alone does not settle conflicting claims.

Record which correction supersedes which result and why. Retain useful negative
results, failed runs, and valid partial arguments with their limits. A failed
route does not falsify the whole question without the connecting argument.

At a checkpoint, update only the changed conclusion, its evidence, remaining
gaps, and next authorized action. Close a study explicitly when appropriate;
an old next-step note does not reopen its compute budget or authorize a new run.
